Lesson 1 of 3

Perimenopause and diagnosis

Menopause is 12 months without a period. Perimenopause, the years before it, brings irregular cycles, hot flashes and night sweats, poor sleep, mood changes, brain fog, joint aches, and vaginal dryness.

  • In women 45 and older with typical symptoms, diagnosis is clinical. FSH swings widely in perimenopause, so a single value is unreliable.
  • Under 40: evaluate for primary ovarian insufficiency (FSH on two occasions, plus TSH, prolactin and hCG). These patients generally need hormone therapy until the average age of menopause.
  • Contraception is still needed in perimenopause, until 12 months without periods after 50, or 24 months before 50.
  • Bleeding after menopause always needs evaluation of the endometrium.
Lesson 2 of 3

Candidacy and risks

The Menopause Society's 2022 position: for people under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, without contraindications, the benefits of hormone therapy generally outweigh the risks for bothersome vasomotor symptoms and for preventing bone loss.

RiskWhat we know
Breast cancerEstrogen plus progestin: small increase with longer use (WHI: under 1 extra case per 1,000 women per year). Estrogen alone after hysterectomy did not increase risk in WHI.
Blood clots and strokeHigher with oral estrogen. Transdermal estradiol appears to carry lower risk in observational data.
Heart diseaseStarting near menopause appears neutral or favorable; starting 10+ years later or after 60 carries more risk.
Endometrial cancerUnopposed estrogen raises risk. Anyone with a uterus needs a progestogen.

Contraindications: unexplained vaginal bleeding, breast cancer or another estrogen-sensitive cancer, prior blood clot or stroke, coronary disease, active liver disease, and known high-risk thrombophilia.

Lesson 3 of 3

Prescribing and monitoring

OptionTypical startNotes
Transdermal estradiol patch0.025 to 0.05 mg/dayPreferred with clot risk, migraine, or high triglycerides
Oral estradiol0.5 to 1 mg/daySimple and inexpensive
Micronized progesterone100 mg nightly (continuous) or 200 mg for 12 days a monthFor endometrial protection; may help sleep
Low-dose vaginal estrogenCream, tablet or ringFor genitourinary symptoms; minimal absorption; no progestogen needed; can continue long term
  • Dose by symptoms, not blood levels. Routine estradiol levels aren't needed.
  • Use FDA-approved products. FDA-approved estradiol and progesterone are bioidentical. Compounded hormones and pellets are not FDA-approved, vary in dose, and pellets can produce very high levels.
  • Testosterone for women has one evidence-based use: hypoactive sexual desire disorder after menopause, at doses appropriate for women, with monitoring.
  • Duration is individualized. There's no automatic stop at 65; reassess yearly with mammography and a risk review.
  • Non-hormonal options: fezolinetant (needs liver tests before and during treatment), low-dose paroxetine and other SSRIs or SNRIs, gabapentin, and CBT for hot flashes and sleep.

Clinical pearls

  • Don't order FSH to diagnose perimenopause in a woman over 45 with typical symptoms.
  • Every patient with a uterus who takes systemic estrogen needs a progestogen.
  • Vaginal estrogen is low-risk and underused for urinary and vaginal symptoms.

Red flags: refer or work up first

  • Any postmenopausal bleeding, or heavy or prolonged bleeding on hormone therapy.
  • A new breast lump, or calf pain, chest pain, or shortness of breath on therapy.
  • Menopause symptoms before 40: evaluate for primary ovarian insufficiency.

Case

A 51-year-old with 10 hot flashes a day, poor sleep and a last period 8 months ago has migraine without aura and a BMI of 33. She has her uterus. She asks about hormone therapy.

Show discussion

She is within 10 years of menopause and under 60, so hormone therapy is reasonable. Transdermal estradiol is preferred given migraine and higher BMI. Add micronized progesterone for endometrial protection. Confirm mammography is current, discuss that she still needs contraception for now, and follow up in about 3 months to adjust the dose.

Patient handout · plain language

Perimenopause and menopause

Perimenopause can start in your 40s. Periods change, and hot flashes, night sweats, poor sleep, mood swings and vaginal dryness are common. You don't have to just put up with it.

  • Hormone therapy is safe and effective for many women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause. We'll go over your own risks.
  • Other options include non-hormone medicines and therapy for sleep and hot flashes.
  • Vaginal estrogen is low-dose and helps dryness and urinary symptoms.
  • You can still get pregnant in perimenopause. Keep using birth control until we tell you it's safe to stop.

Call us about any bleeding after your periods have stopped for a year, a new breast lump, or leg swelling or chest pain.
