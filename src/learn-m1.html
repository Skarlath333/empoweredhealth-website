Learning objectives

  • Describe how upstream factors (sleep, diet, stress, exposures) drive common chronic presentations.
  • Build a problem list that pairs each diagnosis with its likely contributors.
  • Explain where root-cause care adds to, and doesn't replace, guideline-based treatment.

From diagnosis to drivers

A diagnosis tells you what is happening. Root-cause care adds a second question: why in this patient, and why now? Two patients with stage 1 hypertension may share a label but not a cause. One has untreated sleep apnea, the other a high-sodium diet and heavy alcohol use. Treating the blood pressure is necessary for both; finding the driver changes the rest of the plan.

A practical systems lens

Rather than reviewing organ systems in isolation, map how they interact. A useful working set of upstream domains:

Clinical pearl

Review the medication list as a possible cause, not just a treatment. Long-term metformin can lower B12, PPIs can lower magnesium and B12, and statins are associated with muscle symptoms. Check before adding a supplement.

Red flags: don't stop at lifestyle

Unintentional weight loss, GI bleeding, new neurologic deficits, chest pain, or age-appropriate screening that's overdue all need the standard workup first.

Patient handout · plain language

What is functional medicine?

Functional medicine asks why a health problem is happening. Your body's systems are connected: poor sleep raises stress hormones, which affect blood sugar, which affects energy and mood. So we look at food, sleep, movement, stress, your environment and your history, and treat those along with your symptoms. It works alongside your regular care, not instead of it.

Try this week: Write down what you ate, how you slept, and your energy from 1 to 10 each afternoon for 3 days. Bring it to your next visit.
