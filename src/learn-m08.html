Lesson 1 of 3

Stress physiology without the hype

Short-term stress responses (cortisol and adrenaline) are healthy. Long-term activation, sometimes called allostatic load, is linked to high blood pressure, poor sleep, weight gain, insulin resistance, and IBS flares.

  • "Adrenal fatigue" is not a recognized diagnosis. A systematic review found no evidence that it exists, and the Endocrine Society advises against it. Salivary cortisol panels sold for it are not validated.
  • Test for real adrenal disease when it's suspected: morning cortisol and ACTH stimulation for adrenal insufficiency (weight loss, low blood pressure, hyperpigmentation, low sodium); late-night salivary cortisol, 1 mg dexamethasone suppression, or 24-hour urine cortisol for Cushing's syndrome.
  • Screen for depression and anxiety. USPSTF recommends depression screening for all adults and anxiety screening for adults under 65. Use PHQ-9 and GAD-7.

Patients describing "burnout" are often describing real exhaustion, depression, poor sleep, or anxiety. Name what you find and treat it.

Lesson 2 of 3

Sleep

Adults need 7 or more hours. Short sleep worsens blood pressure, glucose, appetite and mood.

ProblemFirst-line approachNotes
Chronic insomniaCBT for insomnia (CBT-I): stimulus control, sleep restriction, cognitive workRecommended first by ACP and AASM; digital programs work; sleep hygiene alone isn't enough
Obstructive sleep apneaSTOP-BANG, then home sleep test or polysomnographyTreat with CPAP, oral appliance, weight loss; GLP-1 therapy can help in obesity

Avoid long-term benzodiazepines and Z-drugs, especially in older adults (Beers Criteria). Review medications, alcohol, and caffeine after noon.

Lesson 3 of 3

Mind-body tools

  • Exercise is an effective treatment for depression. A 2024 network meta-analysis found walking or jogging, yoga and strength training all reduced depressive symptoms, with larger effects at higher intensity.
  • Mindfulness-based stress reduction was as effective as escitalopram for anxiety disorders in a randomized trial.
  • Slow breathing (around 6 breaths a minute for 5 minutes) is a free, quick tool to lower arousal.
  • Social connection matters. The U.S. Surgeon General has called loneliness a public health concern linked to heart disease, dementia and early death.
  • Supplements: evidence is modest. St. John's wort interacts with many drugs, including birth control. Ashwagandha has been linked to rare liver injury and can affect thyroid levels.

These tools add to, not replace, therapy and medication for moderate to severe depression or anxiety.

Clinical pearls

  • Sleep hygiene handouts alone rarely fix insomnia. Prescribe CBT-I.
  • Check ferritin in every patient with restless legs.
  • When a patient asks about adrenal fatigue, screen for depression, sleep apnea, anemia and thyroid disease, and test for true adrenal disease only if signs point to it.

Red flags: refer or work up first

  • Suicidal thoughts: assess the same day. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is available by call or text.
  • Drowsy driving or witnessed apneas: expedite a sleep evaluation.
  • Weight loss, low blood pressure and salt craving (possible adrenal insufficiency), or easy bruising, purple stretch marks and new hypertension (possible Cushing's).

Case

A 44-year-old teacher says she has "adrenal fatigue." She sleeps 5 hours, wakes at 3 a.m. with racing thoughts, and her PHQ-9 is 12. She brings a salivary cortisol panel.

Show discussion

The panel isn't validated and shouldn't guide treatment. Her PHQ-9 suggests moderate depression, and she has insomnia. Offer CBT-I, discuss therapy and possibly medication for depression, add regular exercise, and check basic labs (CBC, TSH, ferritin). Only test for adrenal disease if she has signs of it.

Patient handout · plain language

Better sleep, less stress

  • Go to bed and get up at the same time every day.
  • Use your bed only for sleep and sex. If you can't sleep after about 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy.
  • Get outside light in the morning, and dim screens in the evening.
  • Skip caffeine after noon and limit alcohol, which breaks up sleep.
  • Move every day. Walking counts and helps mood.
  • Try slow breathing: in for 4, out for 6, for 5 minutes.

Tell us if you snore loudly, feel sleepy while driving, or have felt down or anxious most days for 2 weeks or more.

If you are thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988 right away, any time.
